Laminate is a decorative surface bonded over a manufactured core. Unlike solid wood, it does not give you extra material to sand away, and exposed particleboard edges can swell if soaked. The goal is to create adhesion while keeping the laminate intact.

## Decide whether the piece is worth painting

Paint works best when the laminate is firmly bonded and the core is dry and solid. Repair lifting edges before coating. A swollen, crumbling, or water-damaged core will not become stable under primer.

Check hidden edges or the underside to confirm the material. Thin real-wood veneer and plastic laminate are easy to confuse, but sanding risk and product choices differ.

## 1. Clean off grease and polish

Remove hardware. Wash every paintable surface with a compatible degreasing cleaner, paying attention to hand-contact areas and furniture polish residue. Follow rinsing directions and keep excess liquid away from seams.

Allow the piece to dry completely.

## 2. Scuff without cutting through

Use a fine abrasive with light, even pressure. Your aim is a uniformly dulled surface, not exposed core material. Be especially careful on corners and thin edges where the decorative layer is easiest to break through.

Vacuum and wipe off the dust. The surface should be clean and matte, with no powder remaining.

## 3. Test the coating system

Choose a bonding primer whose technical directions explicitly include laminate, melamine, or comparable slick surfaces. Confirm that your finish paint is compatible with that primer.

Apply the complete system to a hidden prepared area. Let it cure for the time the manufacturer specifies, not merely until it feels dry. Check whether normal handling easily scratches or peels it. No field test guarantees permanent adhesion, but an early failure can save you from coating the whole piece with the wrong system.

## 4. Prime and paint thinly

Apply a smooth, complete primer coat without heavy ridges. Respect the recoat window. If sanding between coats is permitted, use a very light touch and remove all residue.

Apply thin finish coats with a good synthetic brush or smooth-surface roller. Watch lower edges for buildup. Do not keep rolling paint as it becomes tacky.

## 5. Protect the cure

Laminate projects are most vulnerable when they appear finished but the coating is still soft. Keep doors and drawers from touching painted edges, reinstall hardware gently, and avoid heavy use until the full cure period has passed.

Even a well-prepared painted laminate surface can chip under sharp impacts. Save matching paint and your product notes for future touch-ups.

## Mistakes that cause early peeling

- Skipping degreasing.
- Sanding through the decorative layer.
- Using a general-purpose primer that does not list slick surfaces.
- Testing adhesion after only a few hours.
- Applying a heavy coat to “make it durable.”
- Putting the piece into service before cure.
