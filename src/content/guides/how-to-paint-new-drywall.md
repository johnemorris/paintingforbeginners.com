New drywall can look deceptively ready for paint. The paper face and joint compound are porous, and unfinished seams or sanding scratches can become much more obvious after coating.

Treat new drywall as a new surface—not as a normal repaint.

## 1. Make sure the drywall finishing is actually finished

Primer and paint do not replace drywall finishing.

Inspect:

- seams
- screw or nail dimples
- inside and outside corners
- patches
- sanding scratches
- ridges at the edge of joint compound

Shine a work light across the wall at a low angle. This makes shadows from humps and depressions easier to see.

Correct defects before coating.

## 2. Remove sanding dust

Drywall dust is extremely fine. Remove it carefully so the primer contacts the surface rather than sitting on loose powder.

Avoid soaking unfinished drywall with water. Use an appropriate vacuum/dust-removal method and follow the primer manufacturer's surface-preparation directions.

## 3. Prime new drywall

Use a primer intended for new drywall or another primer specifically recommended by the finish-paint manufacturer for the substrate.

Primer helps create a more uniform surface across the drywall paper and joint compound and prepares the wall for finish paint.

"Paint and primer in one" does not automatically mean bare drywall should skip the primer specified for new drywall.

## 4. Inspect again after priming

Primer often reveals defects you missed.

After it dries, look for:

- visible seams
- rough patches
- pinholes
- raised fibers
- sanding scratches

Correct small defects as appropriate, remove dust, and re-prime repaired bare areas before finish painting.

## 5. Apply the finish paint

Cut in manageable sections and roll while the edge paint is still wet enough to blend.

Use the roller cover and application rate recommended for the paint and wall texture. Keep a wet edge and avoid repeatedly rolling paint that has started to set.

## How many finish coats?

Plan around the paint manufacturer's directions and the appearance of the wall rather than assuming primer counts as your finish coat.

Two uniform finish coats are common when appearance, color depth, or durability requires them, but product and color matter.

## Why does new drywall sometimes look patchy?

Common reasons include:

- skipping appropriate primer
- uneven joint-compound finishing
- drywall dust left behind
- different absorption between repaired and unrepaired areas
- uneven finish-paint thickness
- trying to correct partly dried paint with the roller

If the wall looks uneven, diagnose the cause before simply piling on more paint.

## Beginner shortcut to avoid

Do not rush from sanding directly into finish paint because the wall is "brand new."

New drywall is one of the surfaces where proper preparation and primer can save you from chasing visible seams and uneven absorption later.
