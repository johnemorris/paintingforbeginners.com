A roller will create some normal movement of paint, but it should not be spraying the room with droplets.

Excessive splatter usually points to speed, loading, roller-cover choice, overworking, or an improperly altered coating.

## Slow down first

Rolling very quickly can throw paint from the cover.

Use controlled passes. You are trying to transfer and distribute a uniform film, not spin the roller as fast as possible.

## Check whether the roller is overloaded

A properly loaded roller should carry paint without pouring or throwing it.

If paint is dripping heavily from the cover or collecting at the ends, distribute the load in the tray or screen before going to the wall.

Correct loading avoids both dry rolling and overloading. Use the visual clues in [how to know whether you put on enough paint](/how-do-i-know-if-i-put-on-enough-paint/).

## Use a cover appropriate for the coating and surface

The wrong nap or a poor-quality cover can contribute to splatter and a poor finish. Check the paint manufacturer's applicator recommendation and [choose the roller nap](/how-to-choose-roller-nap/) for the actual surface.

## Stop overworking paint

Repeatedly rolling paint after it has begun to set can create defects as well as splatter.

Work in manageable sections, maintain a wet edge, and move on once the film has been distributed and lightly finished. The [wet-paint workability test](/should-you-go-back-over-wet-paint/) helps you know when to stop touching the coat.

## Do not casually thin wall paint

Adding water or another thinner without product instructions changes how the coating behaves and can increase problems. Follow the label if thinning is permitted or required.

## Protect the room anyway

Even good roller technique can produce occasional droplets. Protect floors, furniture, fixtures, and adjacent surfaces before rolling.

The fix for excessive splatter is not to squeeze the roller drier and create a thin coat. Load it correctly, slow the pass, use the right cover, and stop reworking paint that is setting.
