Stencil painting transfers a repeated shape through cut openings. Crisp results depend less on pushing paint into every corner than on keeping the stencil flat and the applicator almost dry. Build color gradually.

## Choose a suitable surface and paint

Stencil on a clean, dry, stable surface. Walls should have sound, adequately cured paint. Wood and furniture need the preparation and coating system appropriate to their finish. Canvas should be supported so pressure does not make it bounce. Fabric requires paint or medium intended for that fiber and washing expectations.

Use paint compatible with the base. Test both paint and attachment method in an inconspicuous area. Low-tack tape or adhesive can still lift uncured, weak, chalky, or poorly bonded paint.

## Position the stencil

Measure from a level reference rather than assuming floors, ceilings, and furniture are square. Mark a light baseline or use low-tack tape. For a repeating stencil, identify registration marks and plan where partial repeats will fall before starting.

Secure the stencil so its bridges and small details contact the surface. Repositionable stencil adhesive can help on appropriate surfaces; apply it as directed and prevent residue. Tape the outer edges as additional insurance, not as a substitute for flat contact.

## Load less paint than feels necessary

Dip only the ends of a stencil brush, sponge, or roller, then remove most paint onto a towel or scrap. Test the tool: it should leave a light, controlled mark rather than a wet ridge.

Several light passes are safer than one opaque wet pass. Paint pushed under the stencil has nowhere to go except outward.

## Brush, sponge, or roller?

- **Stencil brush:** use a vertical stippling motion or gentle controlled swirl when the stencil and surface allow it. A nearly dry brush gives excellent control.
- **Dense sponge:** dab vertically. It works well for irregular texture but can create bubbles if overloaded.
- **Small foam roller:** useful for larger repeated wall patterns. Unload it evenly and roll with light pressure; a saturated roller quickly causes bleed.

Avoid brushing hard from an opening toward its edge. Sideways force drives wet paint beneath the stencil.

## Paint a stencil step by step

1. Prepare and fully dry the base surface.
2. Practice the entire sequence on scrap with the same texture.
3. Measure, level, and secure the stencil.
4. Load the applicator, then unload it thoroughly.
5. Apply a light first layer with vertical pressure.
6. Build opacity gradually without flooding edges.
7. Lift the stencil straight away according to the paint and stencil guidance, before buildup bonds it to the surface.
8. Let the design dry before aligning an overlapping repeat.

If a bridge lifts, hold it with a clean fingertip or stencil tool while keeping clear of wet paint.

## Layer colors without smearing

Allow the first color to dry before placing another stencil over it unless intentional wet blending is part of the design. Use registration marks and temporary guides. Begin with broad background shapes and add small foreground details last.

For shaded motifs, use a small amount of a darker mixture at one edge with a nearly dry brush. Do not keep adding wet paint in pursuit of a perfectly smooth gradient.

## Clean and store stencils

Clean reusable stencils before paint hardens, using the method recommended for the stencil material and paint. Support delicate bridges instead of scrubbing across them. Do not use a solvent that can warp or dissolve the stencil.

Dry flat and store flat or in a protective sleeve. Dried paint buildup can change the opening size and prevent close contact on the next project.

## Fix and prevent common mistakes

- **Bleeding edges:** use less paint, less pressure, and better stencil contact.
- **Fuzzy pattern on texture:** accept that deep texture limits sharpness, choose a simpler design, and use a dry brush.
- **Paint lifts with stencil:** the base may be uncured or poorly bonded, or the adhesive too strong.
- **Visible repeat errors:** use registration marks and check level every few repeats.
- **Thick ridges:** apply thinner layers and clean buildup from the stencil.
- **Smears during repositioning:** wipe the stencil back and let adjacent repeats dry.

Perfectly crisp edges are not always possible on rough surfaces. A small test reveals whether the design should be simplified before the full project.
