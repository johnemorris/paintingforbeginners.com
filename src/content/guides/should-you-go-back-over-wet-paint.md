If you just painted an area and notice a small flaw, there is a strong temptation to keep brushing or rolling it until it looks perfect.

Sometimes that works. Sometimes it makes the spot worse.

The difference is whether the paint is still **workable** or has already started to set.

## If the paint is still fresh and moving easily

If you just applied the paint and the area is still fully wet, you can usually blend it with the surrounding wet paint.

Use a light touch. Correct the problem and move on.

Do not keep making pass after pass simply because wet paint does not look perfectly smooth yet. Many paints level as they dry.

## If the paint has started getting sticky, leave it alone

Once your brush or roller starts dragging through the paint instead of moving smoothly, stop.

Going back over partially drying paint can create rough texture, brush or roller marks, ridges, uneven sheen, or areas where the applicator pulls at paint that had already begun to set.

## What if you see a thin or patchy spot?

If the paint is still fresh and you can blend the spot into the surrounding wet area without dragging, you may be able to correct it.

If the surrounding paint is already setting, resist the urge.

Let the coat dry for the time specified by the manufacturer, look at it again under normal lighting, and decide whether it actually needs another coat. The guide to [roller marks, lap lines, and patches](/fix-roller-marks-and-patchy-paint/) helps separate temporary drying differences from dried defects.

## What if you see a drip or run?

A fresh drip that has literally just formed may sometimes be spread out while all the surrounding paint is still workable.

But if fixing it requires dragging through paint that is already setting, stop. Let the coating dry according to its instructions, correct the defect after it is ready to be worked or recoated, and apply paint again as needed. Follow the more specific repair process for [paint drips and runs](/how-to-fix-paint-drips-and-runs/).

## A simple beginner test

Pay attention to how the applicator feels.

**Still gliding through fully wet paint?** You probably have a short opportunity to blend the area.

**Starting to drag, pull, or leave new texture?** Stop.

Sometimes the best painting technique is knowing when to put the brush or roller down. When another full coat is needed, wait for the product's recoat interval and [apply that coat uniformly](/how-many-coats-of-paint/).
