A nail hole or tiny dent can often be filled directly. Once the damage becomes larger, broken, unsupported, or irregular, you need a drywall repair, not simply a thicker blob of spackle.

The exact repair method depends on the hole size and what remains behind it.

## First decide whether this is still a DIY patch

Stop and investigate before patching if the damage involves:

- active water or moisture;
- mold-like growth;
- electrical or plumbing damage;
- a large area of soft drywall;
- structural movement;
- repeated cracking; or
- a hole that exposes something you do not understand.

Repair the cause before repairing the appearance.

## Small-but-larger holes

For damage larger than a simple nail or dent repair but still within the limits of an appropriate drywall patch product, a mesh or self-adhesive patch system may be suitable.

Follow the patch manufacturer's size limits and instructions. Typically the finished repair needs compound feathered beyond the patch, dried, sanded smooth, and prepared for paint.

## Larger openings may need new drywall

A larger opening often needs to be cut back to sound drywall, supported properly, fitted with a drywall piece, taped, and finished with joint compound.

That is a different job from filling a hole. If you are not comfortable identifying framing, utilities, or proper support, this is a reasonable point to bring in someone experienced.

## Feather the repair, do not build a mound

A patch can be structurally sound and still look terrible after painting if the compound ends abruptly.

The finishing coats should transition gradually into the surrounding wall. Once dry, sand only enough to create a smooth transition without damaging the surrounding drywall paper. Remove sanding dust.

## Prime the repair

Fresh joint compound is more porous than an already painted wall. If you paint directly over a large porous patch, it can absorb the finish differently and remain visible.

Prime the repaired area as required before applying the finish paint. Unsealed repairs are a common cause of [paint that looks shiny or dull in spots](/why-does-paint-look-shiny-or-dull-in-spots/).

For genuinely small, supported damage, use the simpler [nail-hole and small drywall repair](/patch-nail-holes-and-small-drywall-damage/).
