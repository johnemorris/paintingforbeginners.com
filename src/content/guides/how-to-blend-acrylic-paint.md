Acrylic blending becomes much easier when you stop trying to rescue a large area that is already drying.

The basic strategy is to prepare first, work a manageable area, overlap colors while both are workable, and stop when the surface begins to drag.

## Mix before you start

Have the colors you need on the palette before painting the blend. If you stop halfway through to search for a tube or mix a missing color, the first area may begin setting.

For a simple two-color blend, premix one or two intermediate colors. That reduces the amount of mixing you must accomplish on the canvas.

## Work smaller

A beginner often makes blending harder by trying to cover a huge area at once.

Paint a manageable band of the first color, add the neighboring color while the edge is still workable, then use a clean or appropriately loaded brush to soften the transition.

## Use enough paint

Trying to blend an almost dry, scrubbed-out film gives you very little working time. Load enough paint to create a workable layer without piling on a heavy mass.

A slightly damp brush can help normal handling, but flooding the area with water is not the same thing as extending acrylic's open time.

## Stop when the surface changes

At some point the paint stops moving smoothly. The brush begins to drag, lift paint, or create rough texture. That is your signal to stop.

Let the layer dry and continue with another layer rather than repeatedly scrubbing half-dry acrylic.

## If you need more working time

Acrylic manufacturers offer slow-drying mediums and related products designed to extend workability. They are optional, not a requirement for learning to blend.

Before buying one, improve the basic workflow: prepare colors, use an appropriately sized brush, work smaller, and do not overwork setting paint. Keeping [palette colors workable](/how-to-keep-acrylic-paint-wet-on-palette/) also gives you more time for planned intermediate mixtures.
