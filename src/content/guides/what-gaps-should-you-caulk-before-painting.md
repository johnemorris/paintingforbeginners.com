Caulk can make painted trim look finished, but “I see a gap” is not enough information to decide that it should be caulked.

For ordinary interior painted trim, a small irregular joint between trim and the wall is a common caulk location. Other gaps may be intentional, may move too much, or may indicate a repair problem.

## Common interior cosmetic joints

Depending on the construction and product instructions, paintable caulk is commonly used for narrow joints such as:

- baseboard to painted wall;
- painted casing to painted wall; and
- small trim-to-trim joints intended to read as one finished assembly.

The caulk should be compatible with the materials and intended conditions.

## Gaps that deserve a second look

Pause before caulking:

- a large or growing gap;
- a joint that repeatedly cracks;
- wet or stained areas;
- gaps around components that need drainage or movement;
- flooring movement joints;
- exterior joints you do not understand; or
- a separation caused by loose trim.

Caulk can hide a symptom without fixing the reason the pieces are separating.

## Caulk is not structural repair

If trim is loose, fasten or repair the trim appropriately first. Do not ask a bead of caulk to hold construction together.

If a gap is unusually large, determine why before trying to bridge it cosmetically.

## Caulk before the finish coat

For a paintable joint, complete the caulking at the preparation stage and allow it to become paint-ready according to its instructions before applying the finish coat.

Do not assume every caulk can be painted immediately—or painted at all.
