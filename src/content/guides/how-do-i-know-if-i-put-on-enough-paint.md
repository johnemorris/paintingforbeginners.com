You have enough paint on the surface when the coat is spreading evenly and your brush or roller can do its job without being forced.

That does **not** mean every first coat must completely hide what is underneath.

Some colors and surfaces need another coat. Trying to force perfect coverage by piling on more wet paint can create runs, ridges, and uneven drying.

## Signs you may be applying too little paint

Watch for a roller that sounds or feels dry and needs hard pressure, a brush that drags instead of flowing, dry-looking patches, very thin streaks, or constantly stretching one load farther than it wants to go.

If your applicator has stopped releasing paint easily, reload it. Do not compensate by pressing harder and harder.

## Signs the amount is about right

A properly applied coat generally looks wet and reasonably even as you work.

Your applicator should move without excessive pressure.

You may still see some of the previous color through the first coat. What matters during application is that the coat itself is being laid down consistently.

## Signs you may be applying too much

A coat may be too heavy if you see runs or drips forming, paint sagging downward, heavy ridges, paint pooling, or an unusually thick wet layer that moves after you apply it.

More paint is not always better. If a heavy area has already started to set, use the [workable-versus-setting test](/should-you-go-back-over-wet-paint/) before touching it again.

## Do not judge coverage too early

Paint changes while it dries. A wet coat can look different from a dry one, and partially drying areas can make a surface look temporarily patchy.

Unless you have an obvious problem that can still be corrected while everything is fresh, let the coat dry according to the manufacturer's instructions before deciding it failed.

## What if I can still see through the first coat?

That alone does not mean you applied it incorrectly.

The right answer may simply be another coat after the first is ready to recoat. A controlled first coat followed by another proper coat is usually better than one very heavy coat intended to hide everything immediately.

If the dried old color still shows, use the [coverage troubleshooting guide](/why-isnt-my-paint-covering-old-color/) to check film thickness, color change, porosity, mixing, and primer needs. The goal is not to put on the most paint possible. The goal is to leave an even coat and let each coat do its job.
