Clean reusable brushes and rollers before the paint dries in them. The correct cleaning liquid depends on the coating, so check the paint label first.

For common water-based wall paint, cleanup is usually water-based. Oil-based coatings may require the solvent specified by the manufacturer.

## Remove excess paint first

Do not begin by sending a fully loaded brush or roller under the faucet.

Work out as much usable paint as practical. A brush can be wiped or worked against the inside of the working container. A roller can be scraped gently with an appropriate roller-cleaning tool.

This makes cleanup faster and keeps unnecessary paint out of the waste stream.

## Cleaning water-based paint from a brush

For a water-cleanup coating:

1. Remove excess paint.
2. Rinse the bristles as directed by the coating manufacturer.
3. Work a small amount of mild soap through the bristles if needed.
4. Rinse until the remaining paint and soap are removed.
5. Reshape the bristles.
6. Let the brush dry before storing it.

Avoid leaving the brush standing in water for long periods. Water above the bristles can damage the handle and ferrule.

## Cleaning a roller cover

Remove excess paint first, then wash the cover according to the coating directions. Work water through the nap until paint stops releasing.

A quality cover can often be reused if it cleans thoroughly and the fibers remain in good condition. If the nap is matted, shedding, hardened, or permanently contaminated, replacement may make more sense than heroic cleaning.

## Oil-based coatings are different

Do not assume water will clean every paint.

If the label specifies mineral spirits or another solvent, follow the product's cleanup and safety directions, including ventilation, personal protection, storage, and disposal requirements.

Never improvise by mixing solvents or pouring them down a drain.

## Dry before long-term storage

After cleaning, remove excess water and allow tools to dry thoroughly. Reshape brush bristles before they dry.

Store clean brushes so the bristles are protected rather than crushed. Keep roller covers clean and protected from dust.

## Do I have to clean everything between coats?

Not necessarily. If you are using the same coating again soon, [keeping the brush or roller sealed between coats](/how-to-store-paint-brushes-and-rollers-between-coats/) may be easier than washing it between every coat.

That is a short-term storage problem, not long-term cleaning. Read the cleanup line on the paint can before you start so the end of the project does not become an improvisation.
