You **can** sand between coats of paint, and sometimes it noticeably improves the finish.

That does not mean every bedroom wall needs to be sanded from corner to corner between every coat.

## Sand between coats when you need to remove defects

Light between-coat sanding is useful for:

- dust nibs
- small dried drips
- raised fibers
- minor brush ridges
- rough patches
- a finish where extra smoothness matters, such as some trim or furniture projects

The goal is usually a light leveling pass, not stripping off the coat you just applied.

## Ordinary walls may not need it

If the first wall coat dried smooth, clean, and free of defects—and the product does not require sanding for recoating—you can often proceed with the next coat after the required recoat interval.

Sanding an already acceptable wall can create unnecessary dust.

## Wait until the coating is ready

"Dry to the touch" does not necessarily mean "ready to sand."

Paint that is still soft can gum up the abrasive, roll into little balls, tear, or become polished rather than sanded cleanly.

Follow the coating manufacturer's drying and recoat instructions. More time may be needed in cool or humid conditions.

## Use a fine abrasive and light pressure

For between-coat smoothing, aggressive paper can create scratches that show through the next coat.

Use the abrasive range recommended for the coating and surface. Test an inconspicuous area when uncertain.

## Remove every bit of loose dust

After sanding, vacuum or clean the surface appropriately.

The next coat should bond to the prepared coating—not sanding residue.

## Do you need to re-prime after sanding?

Not if you only lightly scuffed the finish and did not expose a substrate requiring primer.

If you sand through to bare drywall, wood, filler, or another material that needs priming, spot-prime that area as appropriate before the finish coat.

## Older-paint caution

Between-coat sanding of **newly applied paint** is different from sanding through unknown historical coatings.

If your work may disturb old paint in a pre-1978 home, check current EPA lead-safe guidance before sanding.

## A simple rule

Sand because you can identify something that needs smoothing or because the coating system calls for it.

Do not sand merely because somebody said "real painters always sand between coats."
