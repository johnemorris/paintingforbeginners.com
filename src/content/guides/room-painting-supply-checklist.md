A useful checklist begins before the store. Photograph the room, measure the walls and ceiling, identify the surfaces, and note repairs, stains, texture, and access. Those observations determine the products; a generic kit cannot.

## Paint and primer

- **Finish paint:** Match it to the surface, room conditions, desired washability, and sheen. Record the product line, color, and finish.
- **Primer:** Buy it for a specific need—bare material, patches, stains, difficult adhesion, or a major color change—not automatically.
- **Quantity:** Calculate each product separately using its printed coverage and planned coats.

Ask the paint counter to confirm compatibility when primer and finish products differ. Keep technical labels or product pages available for preparation, ventilation, temperature, drying, and cleanup requirements.

## Preparation supplies

- Mild cleaner compatible with the surface.
- Cloths, sponge, and clean rinse water when directed.
- Putty knife and filler suited to the repair.
- Sanding sponge or abrasive in an appropriate grit.
- Vacuum with a brush attachment.
- Scraper only when loose material must be safely removed.
- Compatible caulk for suitable stationary gaps—not moving joints or clearance gaps.

Do not buy aggressive abrasives until you understand the old coating. Older homes can require lead-safe practices rather than ordinary sanding.

## Protection and organization

- Canvas or nonslip floor coverings for work zones.
- Plastic sheeting where liquid protection is needed, secured against slipping.
- Covers for furniture that remains.
- Painter's tape suitable for the surface and removal timing.
- Labeled bags for switch plates and hardware.
- Work light for inspecting repairs and coverage.
- Trash bags and a clean staging area for wet tools.

Protect the path between the work area and cleanup location, not only the room itself.

## Brush and roller tools

- A 2–2½ inch angled brush for edges and trim.
- A 9-inch roller frame for ordinary walls.
- Roller covers matched to the paint and surface texture, with at least one spare for a multi-day job.
- Tray and liners, or a bucket and grid.
- Extension pole long enough to reduce stretching but short enough to control.
- Stir sticks and a paint-can opener.
- A small pouring container if brushing from the main can would be awkward.

Mini rollers are useful for narrow surfaces but inefficient for entire walls. Detail brushes are not substitutes for a normal cutting brush.

## Safe access and personal protection

Use a stable, rated ladder where necessary. Add eye protection for ceiling work and sanding, suitable gloves for cleaning and coating products, and respiratory protection only when selected and used according to the hazard and instructions. Ventilation requirements depend on the product.

Do not improvise access with chairs, boxes, or stacked furniture.

## Cleanup and storage

- Appropriate cleaning materials listed by the coating.
- A brush comb or simple cleaning tool.
- Rags and a place to dry tools safely.
- Labels and marker for leftover paint.
- A small airtight touch-up container when appropriate.
- Local disposal information for unwanted paint and contaminated materials.

Do not pour paint into storm drains. Solvent products, oily materials, and paint sludge can require different handling from ordinary water-based cleanup.

## What not to buy automatically

Skip specialty edging gadgets, oversized multi-tool kits, paint additives, and large packs of disposable liners unless the project gives them a clear job. One dependable brush and correctly chosen roller cover usually matter more than a cart of shortcuts.
