Cutting in is simply painting the edges that your full-size roller cannot reach safely.

Typical places include:

- where a wall meets the ceiling
- beside baseboards and trim
- inside corners
- around windows and doors
- beside built-ins and other obstacles

You do not need a perfectly steady professional hand on your first attempt.

## Use a brush you can control

A quality **2- to 2½-inch angled sash brush** is a comfortable starting point for many beginners.

The angled edge helps you guide a narrower line while the wider body of the brush carries enough paint to keep moving.

## Do not drown the brush

Dip only enough of the bristles to load the working portion of the brush, then remove obvious excess without scraping the brush completely dry.

Too much paint encourages drips. Too little makes the brush drag and tempts you to overwork the coating.

## Start away from the final line

Place the loaded brush slightly away from the ceiling or trim.

Spread some of the paint, then use the narrower edge of the brush to guide paint toward the boundary. This gives you more control than planting a fully loaded brush directly on the finished line.

## Work in manageable lengths

Do not cut in the entire room and then begin rolling if the cut-in will dry long before the roller reaches it.

Work wall by wall—or in smaller sections when necessary—so the roller can overlap the brushed paint while it is still workable.

## Keep the cut-in band narrow

You only need enough brushed area to let the roller approach safely.

A very wide, heavy brushed band can dry with a different texture or film thickness than the rolled wall.

Roll as close to the edge as you can without hitting the adjoining surface.

## What about inside corners?

Paint the corner carefully with the brush, then roll close to it.

If adjacent walls are different colors, let the first color dry/cure enough for the masking or edge method required before attempting the second color.

## Do beginners need painter's tape?

Not always.

Tape is useful when it makes you more comfortable or protects a finished adjoining surface. Freehand cutting can be faster once you gain control.

Use whichever method gives you a clean result without turning the project into a contest.

## Practice before the most visible wall

A closet, wall that will be partly hidden by furniture, or easy lower edge can be a good place to learn how much paint your brush holds.

Within a few sections you will get a much better feel for loading, pressure, and speed.
