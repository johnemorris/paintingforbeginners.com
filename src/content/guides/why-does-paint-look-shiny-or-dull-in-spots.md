If a wall is the right color but looks shinier or duller in certain spots, you may be seeing uneven sheen, often called flashing.

Flashing can happen when different parts of the wall absorb paint differently or when the finish coat is not uniform.

## Common causes

Look for:

- unprimed or unevenly primed drywall repairs;
- porous patches;
- paint applied too thinly in some areas;
- lap marks or uneven film thickness;
- touch-ups applied differently from the original coat;
- temperature, humidity, or moisture differences while paint dried; or
- a surface that was not uniform before the finish coat.

## Check it after the paint dries

Wet paint can look wildly uneven. Do not diagnose flashing while one area is still drying. Wait for the coating's specified drying interval and inspect the wall in normal light and from an angle.

## Repairs are a common culprit

Joint compound and spackle can absorb paint differently from an already painted wall. If those repairs are not sealed or spot-primed appropriately, the finish coat may dry with a different sheen over them.

Properly finishing and priming [small drywall repairs](/patch-nail-holes-and-small-drywall-damage/) helps prevent porous patches.

## Uneven application can do it too

A coat that is thin in one area and heavier in another can reflect light differently. Lost wet edges and lap marks can create similar visible bands.

Do not try to fix this by pressing harder on a nearly dry roller. That usually adds texture without creating a uniform film. Use [controlled roller loading, overlap, and pressure](/how-to-use-a-paint-roller/) instead.

## How to fix it

The correction depends on the cause.

If porous repairs are showing, allow the surface to become ready for recoating, prepare the repair properly, and prime it if needed.

If the underlying surface is uniform but the finish coat itself is uneven, another properly applied full coat may even the appearance.

If moisture or another continuing condition caused the problem, correct that condition before recoating.

## Flashing versus a bad color match

A useful clue is angle. If a patch becomes much more obvious when light strikes the wall from the side, sheen or texture is probably contributing.

That is why [a touch-up can use the correct color and still be visible](/why-does-touch-up-paint-show/).
