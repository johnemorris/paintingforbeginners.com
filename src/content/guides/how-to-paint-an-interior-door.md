A door combines detailed edges with a large, highly visible surface. Good preparation and thin coats matter because excess paint can make a door stick, soften details, or collect around hardware.

## Paint it hanging or remove it?

Painting a door in place requires less lifting and lets you work on both sides without waiting to flip it. Removing it makes the bottom and hinge edge accessible and can improve control, but doors are heavy and must be supported securely.

If removal is awkward, the door is unusually heavy, or hinge alignment is uncertain, leave it installed or get help. Never balance a door loosely on narrow objects.

## 1. Label hardware and inspect contact points

Remove knobs and simple hardware when practical, keeping parts in labeled bags. Do not paint hinges, latches, weather seals, or identification labels. If hardware stays, mask it precisely.

Notice where the door rubs the frame. Paint will not correct a misaligned or swollen door and adds film thickness. Address the underlying fit problem before coating.

## 2. Clean, repair, and dull the finish

Clean especially well around the knob and lower portion of the door. Fill unwanted dents with a compatible product and sand repairs level after they dry.

Scuff a sound glossy coating to a uniform dullness without rounding panel details or cutting through thin veneer. Remove dust thoroughly. Treat old or uncertain coatings with appropriate caution before sanding.

## 3. Prime where the surface calls for it

Prime bare repairs, exposed wood, stains, dramatic color changes, or hard-to-bond finishes using a product intended for the condition. Let it dry and smooth minor raised fibers if the instructions allow.

## 4. Follow the door's construction

For a paneled door, use this order:

1. Recessed panels and their molded edges.
2. Horizontal rails around the panels.
3. Vertical stiles, finishing with long strokes in their direction.

For a flat door, use a smooth-surface roller on the broad face, then lightly finish according to the paint directions and desired texture. Maintain a wet edge and avoid repeatedly rolling paint that has started to set.

Paint hinge and latch edges only when clearance allows and coating them is appropriate. Keep paint out of hardware recesses and off the top and bottom unless the door manufacturer or project condition calls for those edges to be sealed.

## 5. Watch for buildup

Inspect panel corners, the lower edge, and around hardware openings for runs. Correct them while the coating is wet and workable. Thin coats preserve the door profile and cure more predictably than a heavy coat.

Allow the full recoat interval. If the first coat feels rough, lightly smooth it only as permitted and remove all dust before the second.

## 6. Reassemble without damaging soft paint

Let the finish dry adequately before reinstalling hardware. Tighten screws securely but avoid twisting hardware across a soft painted surface. Keep the door open or use non-marking spacers so fresh painted edges do not bond to the frame.

Wait through the manufacturer's cure period before washing or subjecting the door to hard use. If it sticks after curing, identify the exact contact point rather than repeatedly forcing it closed.
