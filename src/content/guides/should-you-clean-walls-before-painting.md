Yes—if the wall has anything on it that could interfere with adhesion or leave visible defects. But that does not mean every wall needs an aggressive scrub with a strong cleaner.

Paint needs a surface that is sound, clean, dry, and compatible. Your job is to remove contamination, not to create a new problem by soaking the wall or leaving cleaner residue behind.

## When cleaning matters most

Clean before painting when you see or suspect:

- cooking grease or oily film;
- fingerprints and body oils around switches and doors;
- smoke or soot;
- dust from repairs or sanding;
- hairspray, cosmetics, or bathroom residue;
- dirt around vents and baseboards;
- sticky residues; or
- unknown contamination.

Kitchen walls, doors, trim, and areas touched frequently deserve particular attention.

## A lightly used wall may need very little

A clean bedroom wall in good condition may need little more than dust removal and spot cleaning. There is no prize for washing a sound wall with the strongest cleaner you can find.

Start mild. Follow the paint manufacturer's surface-preparation instructions, and use a cleaner appropriate for the contamination you actually have.

## Do not paint over grease

Grease is different from ordinary dust. Painting over an oily film can cause poor adhesion and an uneven finish.

If a wall feels slick or has cooking residue, clean it thoroughly with an appropriate degreasing cleaner, following its directions. Rinse if the cleaner requires rinsing, and let the surface dry completely.

## Cleaning does not replace repair

Cleaning will not fix peeling paint, loose drywall paper, water damage, cracks, or failed coatings. Likewise, sanding is not a substitute for cleaning. Sanding greasy contamination can spread it around rather than remove it.

Use the broader [surface-readiness checklist](/surface-preparation-before-painting/) to decide what the wall needs beyond cleaning.

## Let the wall dry

Do not trap cleaning moisture under fresh paint. After cleaning, allow the wall to dry fully under normal room conditions before priming or painting.

If the wall remains damp or moisture keeps returning, stop and find the source rather than painting over it.

## What about mildew?

Visible mildew or recurring moisture deserves more care than ordinary cleaning. Determine why the area stays damp and follow appropriate product and safety guidance. Do not simply cover a biological or moisture problem with paint.

## The beginner rule

You do not need to perform the same washing ritual on every room. Ask: **Is there anything on this surface that I would be asking the new paint to stick to instead of the wall?**

If the answer is yes, remove it first. Then decide separately [whether sanding is needed](/do-you-need-to-sand-before-painting/).
