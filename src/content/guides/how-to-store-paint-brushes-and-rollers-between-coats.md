If you are using the same paint again soon, you usually do not need to wash a brush and roller between every coat. The goal is simply to keep air away from the wet paint long enough that it does not dry in the tool.

For a short break or overnight pause, wrap or seal the brush and roller tightly according to the coating manufacturer's guidance.

## Keep air out

A practical short-term method is to wrap the loaded portion of the brush or roller tightly in plastic wrap or place it in a well-sealed plastic bag with as much excess air removed as practical.

Do not leave the tool exposed in the tray. The outer paint can skin over or dry into the fibers surprisingly quickly.

## Short-term means short-term

This technique is for a brief interruption—not for storing dirty painting tools indefinitely.

If the next use is not soon, [clean the brushes and rollers properly](/how-to-clean-paint-brushes-and-rollers/).

## Keep the paint itself sealed too

Cover the working paint container and tray so they do not skin over, collect dust, or dry out.

For paint that will remain after the project, [seal and label the leftover paint](/how-to-store-leftover-paint/) rather than treating an open tray as storage.

## What about a refrigerator or freezer?

You will encounter all kinds of painter folklore. The safest beginner approach is simpler: use a short-term method supported by the coating or tool manufacturer, keep the tool sealed from air, and clean it if the delay becomes longer than intended.

You do not need a complicated ritual to avoid washing a roller between two coats on the same project.
