Acrylic dries as water leaves the paint. Thin piles spread across a warm, dry palette can skin over quickly.

You cannot keep ordinary acrylic open forever, but you can waste much less paint by changing how much you put out, how you arrange it, and how you manage the palette.

## Put out less paint

Start with smaller amounts and add more as needed.

A beginner often squeezes out every color at the start, then discovers that half of it has dried before it was needed.

## Keep paint in compact piles

A thin smear has much more surface area exposed to air than a compact pile. Mix in a separate working area and leave the main color piles relatively compact.

## Control the environment

Warm temperatures, direct sun, moving air, and low humidity can shorten working time.

Move the palette out of direct sunlight and away from a fan or strong air vent when practical.

## Mist carefully

Some artists lightly mist palette paint to reduce surface drying. Too much water changes paint consistency, so the goal is not to create puddles.

Follow the acrylic manufacturer's guidance for your paint.

## Consider a stay-wet palette

A stay-wet palette uses a moisture reservoir and membrane to help acrylic remain workable longer. It can be useful for longer sessions, color mixing, and blending.

It is optional. For a short beginner session, simply putting out less paint may solve most of the problem.

Keeping colors workable makes planned gradients and intermediate mixes easier when you [blend acrylic paint](/how-to-blend-acrylic-paint/).
