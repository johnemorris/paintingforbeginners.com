Many beginner brush problems come from one of two extremes:

**the brush is dripping wet** or **the brush is almost dry**.

The goal is a loaded brush that releases paint smoothly.

## Do not fill the container to the top

Pour a manageable amount of paint into a small pail or cutting bucket.

This gives you room to load the brush without submerging the handle and ferrule.

## Dip the working part of the bristles

Load roughly the lower portion of the bristles rather than burying the brush all the way to the metal ferrule.

The exact amount varies with brush, paint, and task, but paint packed deeply into the ferrule makes cleanup harder and can encourage mess.

## Remove obvious excess—do not scrape it bone dry

Lightly tap or wipe enough paint from the brush to prevent a drip from falling on the way to the wall.

Avoid repeatedly scraping both sides until hardly any paint remains. A starved brush drags, leaves weak coverage, and encourages excessive brushing.

## Spread first, finish second

Put the loaded brush onto the work slightly away from your precision edge.

Spread the paint across the working area, then make your controlled finishing strokes.

For cutting in, move toward the final edge after you have released some of the load.

## Reload before the brush starts dragging

When the brush stops flowing easily, reload it.

Pressing harder on a nearly dry brush usually makes control worse rather than stretching the paint farther.

## Do not keep brushing paint that is setting

Modern coatings can begin changing quickly after application.

Once the paint is laid out evenly, leave it alone. Repeatedly brushing a partly drying area can leave texture and visible marks.

## If the brush becomes gummy

Paint can accumulate and begin drying in the bristles during a long project.

Follow the brush and coating manufacturer's cleaning guidance. A clean, properly conditioned brush is easier to control than one packed with partly dried paint.

## The feel you are looking for

A properly loaded brush should:

- reach the wall without dripping
- release paint without heavy pressure
- cover a useful distance before reloading
- leave enough material to level according to the coating's design

You will learn that feel faster by paying attention to the brush than by trying to count exact dips.
