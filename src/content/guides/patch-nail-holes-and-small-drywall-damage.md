A fresh coat of paint does not hide most wall damage. In fact, new paint can make ridges, dents, and poorly feathered patches easier to see.

The good news is that the small holes beginners encounter most often are usually straightforward to repair.

## First, decide whether this is really a small repair

This guide is for things such as:

- picture-hanger and nail holes
- small screw or anchor holes
- shallow dents and dings
- small areas where the drywall face paper is damaged but the wall is otherwise sound

Large holes, loose drywall, recurring cracks, water damage, soft material, mold, or damage around electrical/plumbing components deserves a more specific repair before painting.

## Step 1: Remove anything loose

Pull or cut away loose paper and crumbly material without enlarging the repair unnecessarily.

If an old anchor is sticking out, remove it or deal with it according to its type rather than burying a proud edge under filler.

## Step 2: Fill the damage

For a tiny nail hole, press a small amount of spackle into the opening and scrape away the excess.

For a deeper hole or dent, build the repair according to the patching product's directions. Trying to fill a deep defect with one oversized blob can increase shrinking, cracking, and drying time.

Hold the putty knife at a shallow angle and feather the material beyond the damaged area.

## Step 3: Let it dry completely

Do not sand a patch simply because the surface looks dry.

Drying time changes with product, patch depth, temperature, and humidity. Follow the label.

## Step 4: Sand the repair smooth

Use a fine sanding sponge and light pressure. Your goal is to remove ridges and blend the edge of the repair into the wall.

Run your fingertips across the area and shine a light across the wall from the side. Low-angle light reveals humps and edges that overhead lighting can hide.

Avoid aggressive sanding that exposes or fuzzes drywall paper.

> **Older-home caution:** Sanding or disturbing old painted surfaces can create hazardous lead dust. If the home may contain pre-1978 paint, check current EPA lead-safe guidance before disturbing the coating.

## Step 5: Remove the dust

Vacuum or wipe away sanding dust using a method appropriate for the surface. Paint and primer should go onto a clean repair, not a layer of powder.

## Step 6: Spot-prime the repair

Patching compounds can absorb paint differently from the surrounding painted wall. That can leave a dull or differently textured spot called flashing.

Spot-prime exposed patching material and damaged drywall before applying the finish paint.

## Will the patch disappear after one coat?

Maybe, but do not count on paint to finish the repair for you.

If you can clearly feel a ridge before painting, you will probably still see it afterward. Fix the shape of the patch first; then use primer and finish paint to make the repaired area visually consistent.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not keep adding filler to make the repair "stronger." For small cosmetic wall damage, extra material often creates a larger sanding problem.

Use enough material to fill and feather the defect, then stop.
