Acrylic is forgiving on the canvas and unforgiving in a forgotten brush.

The habit that matters most is simple: **do not set down a paint-loaded acrylic brush and forget about it.**

## Rinse during the session

Keep clean water nearby and rinse a brush when:

- changing to a color that needs a clean brush;
- switching to another brush for more than a brief moment;
- paint is beginning to build near the ferrule; or
- the bristles start feeling sticky or stiff.

After rinsing, blot or wipe away excess water before loading paint again.

## Do not store the brush bristle-down in water

Leaving brushes standing in water can bend the bristles and allow water to affect the ferrule and handle.

Rinse the brush, then lay it down safely or keep it in active use.

## Keep paint away from the ferrule

You do not need to bury the entire bristle bundle in paint. Repeatedly packing acrylic high into the ferrule makes thorough cleaning harder.

Load the working portion of the brush and rinse before buildup dries.

## Clean at the end of the session

Do not postpone final cleaning until tomorrow. Remove excess paint, rinse, wash as appropriate for the brush and paint, reshape the bristles, and allow the brush to dry.

Once acrylic hardens deeply in the bristles, restoration becomes much harder and may not fully return the brush to its original condition.

A slightly damp working brush and regular rinsing are different from soaking. Use [the acrylic brush-moisture guide](/how-wet-should-acrylic-paint-brush-be/) to keep the stroke under control.

A second water container can be useful: one for the first dirty rinse and another for a cleaner final rinse. It is inexpensive and makes color changes easier.
