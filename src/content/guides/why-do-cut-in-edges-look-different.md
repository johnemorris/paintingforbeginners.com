You paint the edges with a brush, roll the middle, and after drying there is a darker or shinier "frame" around the wall.

This is a known application problem sometimes called **picture framing** or **hatbanding**.

## Why the border can look different

Brushing and rolling do not automatically deposit paint at the same thickness or texture.

The difference becomes more visible when:

- the cut-in is applied heavily
- the roller coat is thin
- the brushed band is very wide
- the cut-in dries before the roller overlaps it
- the paint is not mixed thoroughly
- the surface absorbs paint unevenly

## Keep the cut-in narrow

Brush only as far from the edge as necessary to give the roller a safe working margin.

Then bring the roller close enough to overlap some of that brushed area without striking the ceiling or trim.

## Do not cut in the whole room too early

Work in manageable sections.

If the cut-in dries completely before you roll the wall, the overlap may behave more like an additional layer than a blended transition.

## Apply the roller coat uniformly

Do not compensate for a heavy cut-in by pressing harder on a nearly dry roller.

Keep the roller properly loaded and apply a consistent film across the wall.

## Mix the paint thoroughly

If components have settled, the paint at one stage of the job may not match the paint used later.

Stir according to the product directions, especially after the can has been sitting.

## Can another coat fix it?

Often, a properly applied uniform coat can greatly improve picture framing once the previous coat is ready for recoating.

Use the corrected technique across the entire affected wall rather than painting only a narrow strip over the visible border.

## What if the wall is already dry and the border is still obvious?

Check whether the issue is only color/sheen or whether there is also a physical ridge from heavy brush application.

If there is a ridge, smooth it appropriately before recoating. If the surface is smooth, concentrate on uniform application, narrow cut-ins, roller overlap, and wet-edge timing.

The goal is not to eliminate the brush. It is to make the brush and roller behave like parts of the same coat.
