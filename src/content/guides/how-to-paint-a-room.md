Painting a room is mostly a preparation and organization job. The actual rolling goes quickly when the walls are clean, the repairs are smooth, and your tools are already in the room.

## Before you buy paint

Choose a wall paint intended for the room. Flat and matte finishes hide small wall flaws but are harder to scrub. Eggshell is a forgiving all-purpose choice for many living spaces. Bathrooms and hard-working kitchens usually benefit from a more washable finish recommended for that environment.

Check the paint can for coverage and drying instructions. Strong color changes, patched walls, stains, glossy surfaces, or bare drywall may require primer. When in doubt, paint a small test area and let it dry fully before committing.

## 1. Empty and protect the room

Move small items out. Shift large furniture toward the middle and cover it. Remove switch plates, outlet covers, curtain hardware, and anything else that makes you paint around awkwardly. Keep screws with their parts in labeled bags.

Cover the floor completely in the work area. Canvas drop cloths stay put well; plastic protects against liquid but can become slippery. Tape down edges that could become trip hazards.

## 2. Repair, sand, and clean

Fill nail holes and shallow dents with spackle. Let repairs dry, then sand until you cannot feel a ridge when you pass your fingertips across the wall. Remove dust afterward.

Walls collect more residue than they appear to. Wipe away dust, cobwebs, grease, and marks with a cleaner compatible with the paint. In kitchens, pay extra attention near the stove. Let the walls dry before priming or painting.

> **Beginner warning:** Paint does not hide raised patches, peeling edges, or grit. It often makes them easier to see.

## 3. Tape only where it helps

Painter's tape can protect baseboards, fixed hardware, and surfaces you are not comfortable cutting around. Press the edge down firmly on a clean, dry surface. Tape is not a substitute for careful brushing, and leaving it on too long can make removal harder. Follow its label.

## 4. Stir the paint and work in sections

Stir from the bottom until the color and consistency are even. If you have several cans of the same color, combining them in a larger bucket can reduce small batch differences.

Start at the top. Paint a 2–3 inch band along the ceiling, corners, and trim with the angled brush. Work on one wall or manageable section instead of cutting in the whole room far ahead of the roller.

## 5. Load and use the roller

Dampen a water-compatible roller cover very lightly, then remove excess water. Load it evenly with paint; it should be full but not dripping. Roll a section roughly 3 feet wide in overlapping passes. Finish with light strokes in the same direction to even the texture.

Do not press hard to squeeze out the last paint. A dry roller creates thin coverage and heavy pressure leaves ridges. Reload sooner.

Keep a wet edge by moving into the next section before the previous edge dries. Avoid repeatedly rolling an area once it starts to set—it can pull up paint and leave a rough patch.

## 6. Let the coat dry, then inspect

Use the recoat time on the label, not a guess based on whether the surface feels dry. Look across the wall in daylight and with the room lights on. A second coat usually improves color depth, sheen consistency, and touch-up blending.

Before the final coat becomes hard, carefully score any paint bridging onto tape and pull the tape back over itself at an angle. Product directions differ, so follow the tape and paint labels.

## 7. Put the room back carefully

Remove tools and coverings, reinstall dry switch plates, and give the walls time to cure before scrubbing them or pressing furniture against them. Dry paint can still be soft. The label's cure time matters more than the clock.

## Common first-room mistakes

- Buying paint before measuring the walls.
- Skipping cleaning because the wall “looks fine.”
- Using the same roller cover for every texture.
- Painting over wet filler or damp walls.
- Stretching paint too thin to avoid opening another can.
- Touching up partially dried paint until it becomes lumpy.

If a coat looks uneven while wet, pause. Let it dry completely before deciding what needs correction.

## Cleanup

Scrape excess paint from tools back into the container. Clean water-based paint from brushes and rollers according to the label and local wastewater guidance; never pour paint into a storm drain. Seal leftover paint, label it with the room and date, and store it where it will not freeze or overheat. Follow local rules for unwanted paint.
